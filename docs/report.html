1. Where we stand

emerald-square

What to do, in order

  1. Memory: when it runs out, the engine stops instead of showing a coarser image; and its cap (288 MB) is hard-coded, the same on every machine. Critical, first.
  2. Sun shadows: 4.7 ms of 10.7 ms from the street. Keep them from frame to frame instead of redrawing.
  3. Geometry: 48 bytes per triangle, 6× Unreal (8.7), versus 73 for Three which keeps the whole glTF, tangents included. Compress at cook time, like Unreal. And twelve copies of the model = 243 MB: one copy, the instance as an index.
  4. Textures: 6.6 GB in memory, uncompressed. Unreal compresses and sets a pool.
  5. From the street, the engine matches Three.js (10.6 ms vs 11.1 ms): even with no light, its base costs 5.9 ms. The full-screen materials pass is next to measure.

One bar per engine and per question: Three.js vanilla (plain draw, no tricks), Three.js LOD (the classic method: three detail levels per object), Web Geometry, Unreal (its published numbers, on its console). Shorter bar = better. Green = good, yellow = same as Three, red = a problem.

overview: faster than Three (38.6×) · from the street: faster than Three (11.2×)

How many triangles are drawn per frame?

overview: faster than Three (5.8×) · from the street: faster than Three (5.6×)

Not a race: Unreal aims for one triangle per pixel (25 M at 4K). Three draws everything, even hidden; LOD drops distant meshes; we pick by cluster.

How many draw calls per frame?

overview: faster than Three (129.1×) · from the street: faster than Three (47.5×)

A call is one request to the GPU. Fewer is better. The engine issues 18, Three hundreds — LOD does not change that.

faster than Three (9.1×)

And on a small screen (1248×702)?

overview: faster than Three (5.5×) · from the street: faster than Three (2.2×)

How much memory for textures?

same as Three

Everyone keeps the whole city in memory, uncompressed. Unreal sets a pool and compresses. That is the largest gap.

How much memory per triangle?

5× heavier than Unreal

Three: everything the glTF carries (positions, normals, tangents, two UV sets, indices), measured; its LOD levels add their indices. Us: ~48, uncompressed. Unreal: 8.7, everything compressed.

Same layout as Unreal?

yes, same numbers

The engine is built like Unreal, with the same numbers. What is missing is not the structure: it is compression and a bounded pool.

  • 128 triangles per cluster: yes, same.
  • Clusters grouped 8 to 32: yes, same.
  • 128 KiB pages: yes, same.
  • Fixed memory pool: Unreal 512 MB; we count pages, not bytes.

When nothing moves, does the engine still work?

nothing: the frame is held

Three redraws everything, every frame. The engine holds the image and does nothing, like Unreal. On a laptop, that is battery life.
