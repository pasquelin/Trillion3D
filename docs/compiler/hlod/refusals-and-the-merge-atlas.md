# Refusals and the merge atlas

The compiler refuses the job (`INVALID_OPTIONS`, never the whole batch) for a field a layer's
builder does not take, a name given twice, a parent or a rule naming no layer, a cycle, a parent
wearing its sources' own materials above a layer whose actors wear materials of their own, a layer
not spatially loaded naming a cell size or a loading range, a chain past 256 levels — the world
table writes a level in 8 bits —, a match naming nothing, a regular expression that does not
compile, an atlas setting on a `merge` layer that keeps its materials, `bakeVertexColors` on one
that merges them, a sizing's setting beside another sizing (a `merge` layer's `textureSize` beside a
sizing other than `fixed` and `biased` included), a size filter's setting beside another filter,
`opacity` and `opacityMask` both baked, an `extras.hlodInclude` that is no boolean, and values out
of bounds. The HLOD is part of the cache key.

A `merge` layer merging its materials makes one entry per material and what it is baked on: a
full-frame quad of the material, the tile its sources' own coordinates address, or — where the look
depends on the mesh (`bakeOnMesh`), or on what lies behind a translucent material seen through
(`translucency`), whatever `bakeOnMesh` says — that mesh's triangles at coordinates laid once per
mesh: its lightmap texture set where `reuseLightmapUVs` (on by default) finds one, else coordinates
generated by `uvMethod`. Every quad of one material is one entry, so a material is baked once
however many objects wear it; the copies of a mesh mirrored alike share one entry, read behind as
its first copy lies. Each entry is baked at the atlas's side, one sample a texel at its centre, its
textures filtered over the texel: the compiler decides which texel each triangle covers, and the GPU
reads the material there, by the program an `approximate` layer's atlas reads its sources with, on
the compile's one device — its bytes follow that GPU's arithmetic, as a shell's do; an image of one
value shrinks to one texel, the texels no triangle covers take the mean of their neighbours ring by
ring. The entries then fill a grid of `⌈√n⌉` cells a side (or binned squares), each brought down to
its cell less its gutter by a Mitchell–Netravali cubic, the gutters and empty cells filled the same
way; each object's texture coordinates move into its entry's cell.

A mesh's generated coordinates — those of a mesh that has no texture set to reuse, or of a layer
with `reuseLightmapUVs: false`, by default its patches (`uvMethod` `default` or `patchBuilder`;
`xAtlas` cuts and packs its charts instead, as an `approximate` layer's `uvMethod: 'charts'` does;
`legacy` and `uvAtlas` as above) — are unwrapped at fixed settings, whatever the layer: a hundred
initial patches, the normal's turn weighed once, islands merged up to a mean squared area distortion
of 1.5 and a normal turn of 45°. The mesh is first welded and held as a mesh holds one: a triangle
naming a point twice or repeating another is left out and keeps its own first texture set; one on an
edge two triangles hold already gets copies of that edge's points. A layer with
`reuseLightmapUVs: false` unwraps all its entries' triangles as one mesh, once, at these settings
(one packing for all of them): each entry's points are welded among themselves, never with another
entry's. The initial patches are as many seeds as the area holds at one per the mesh's area over the
patch count (at most one per vertex), each component's next seed the triangle furthest from its
seeds so far, every triangle then given to the seed nearest by a distance weighed by its normal's
turn; a patch of one triangle joins a neighbour and a triangle bordered on all its other sides by
one patch moves into it. The patches and the island candidates are ordered by a fixed unstable sort,
so equal scores always fall in the same order. Each island is flattened by an exponential map from
its vertex furthest from its longest boundary loop — the boundary walked as its triangles wind it,
split at a vertex it passes twice —, in a frame laid along the edge that vertex met first, then
turned about its normal to the world's horizontal axes; the map's vertex normals are weighed by area
and interior angle and smoothed five times, each time a quarter of the way towards their
neighbours'. The map is drawn in the axes of a `z`-up mesh, the scene's `y` and `z` exchanged, and
its fronts take vertices in order of their distance in single precision. A whole map — an open
island, every vertex reached — is turned about the centre of its bounding box to its least-area
bounding box, in single precision: the convex hull of its vertices, one entry each in the order its
triangles first name them, gives the directions tried, one per hull edge, and the map turns only
when an edge's box has strictly less area than the axis-aligned one. The islands are then packed
into a square of 512 texels. Where the packer finds no layout — more islands than texels, or islands
of no area — the maps' own coordinates are kept and the mesh is baked all the same, reading what of
them lies in the square. An `approximate` layer's `uvMethod: 'patches'` unwraps its shell the same
way at its own patch settings, then packs the islands again onto its atlas's blocks; there too, a
packing that finds no layout keeps the coordinates as they were.

An `approximate` layer's `uvMethod: 'uvAtlas'` cuts its shell into charts by spectral analysis of
the surface: the shell is held as a mesh, turned over, and cut into as many charts as keep their
stretch under `uvAtlasStretch`, every face's metric the identity; the charts are packed into 512
texels with a 2.5-texel gutter, then packed again onto the atlas's blocks as every method's are. The
shell is not cleaned first: a vertex shared by two fans of triangles makes the cut fail, and a shell
whose cut fails is projected from the faces of its bounding box instead — each triangle onto the
face its normal is nearest, a region of one triangle joining the neighbour that holds more —, then
packed the same way. A `merge` layer's `uvAtlas` unwraps a mesh the same way at a fixed stretch
bound (0.11); a mesh whose cut fails keeps its own first texture set.
